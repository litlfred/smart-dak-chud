The following page provides a reference to resources that can guide countries in adapting the specifications in this IG for country use.

The specifications included in this implementation guide provides the generic, software agnostic and software neutral content requirements. They are documentation of generic system requirements that need to be adapted to local needs; and subsequently used to inform the design of digital systems implemented locally.

WHO guidelines articulate and endorse rigorously tested recommendations for health interventions to be adopted within country programs. When applied correctly and consistently, guideline recommendations save lives. To ensure that countries can effectively benefit from digital health investments, the SMART Guidelines approach is intended to facilitate the adoption of WHO’s clinical, public health and data use guidelines in an accurate way in the digital systems that countries are adopting. For more on layers of knowledge representation and how they are used in WHO’s SMART Guidelines approach, see WHO’s SMART Guidelines and this Lancet article.	

**Strategy and Investment**
- Member States are increasingly looking to build or augment their own digital systems to protect their populations from climate-sensitive health threats;
- Member States will further adapt and extend these specifications to suit their needs, moving from theoretical use cases to localized adaptation;
- Member States have the capacity to maintain normative standards supported by consistent resourcing, ensuring the system evolves beyond initial project lifecycles.


**Infrastructure**
- Member States must have access to reliable national epidemiological surveillance data and WMO-accredited climate data sources;
- Member States possess or are in the process of developing a data integration layer that can ingest, transform, and unify data from both sources.

**Legislation, Policy and Compliance**
- Member States must adhere to their national legal and policy frameworks when implementing a CHUD solution.


**Leadership and Governance**
- A centralized infrastructure provides Member States the ability to enforce standards for data harmonization, ensuring different systems can "speak the same language".
Workforce
- Member States will ensure that surveillance officers, data publishers, and data analysts are trained to collaborate using the common language of the unified system.


**Services and Applications**
- The CHUD solution operates as an ecosystem approach, connecting existing national surveillance systems and digital public infrastructure to enable early warning systems.
